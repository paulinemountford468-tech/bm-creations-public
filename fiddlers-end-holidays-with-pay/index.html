Holidays with Pay

You have to hand it to our gang. If it’s one thing they really like, it’s a treat, and Shamus thinks today is an ideal time for one. It’s a lovely summer morning and looks like being a really hot day.

‘We will have today off,’ he says.

‘A day off what?’ replies the Boss.

‘Well, a day off everything, that’s what I mean.’

‘If you two have finished deciding what’s off or not, perhaps you’ll let us know,’ chirps Captain Wink, ducking out of the Boss’s reach.

‘We will have a day on the beach.’

This seems to be a splendid suggestion, as no sooner was the word beach left from Shamus’ mouth than all sorts of seaside aids were being rooted out. Even Captain Wink had a smile on his face thinking of a plateful of juicy shrimps off Salty Sam’s Seafood stall.

‘Everybody ready?’ chirps Titch, and promptly jumps into the cab of No. 47. With a noise like a huge tin bath full of old cans sliding down a hill, they set off, not even bothering to undo the washing line with Shamus’ vest and pants still fluttering on it.

As the bus passes through Fiddlers End, the inhabitants, recognising all the noise and clatter, seem to disappear like rabbits down a hole, only emerging when our gang are well on their way, breathing a sigh of relief that havoc was not going to be brought to Fiddlers End, well, not for today at least.

As the bus reaches Sillysea, the Boss realises something. The gang are all short of one thing: money. And as an ideal day at the seaside includes ice cream, shrimps, deckchairs, etc., he tells them that they had better come up with a way of earning some money quickly.

Titch sees an ideal spot to park the bus and everyone piles off.

‘Hang on, bring the ticket machine,’ says Shamus. ‘I’ve got an idea to make us a bit of money.’

Off they all head towards the beach, not daring to stop outside one shop, Willy Turt the Tattooist. They all look with envy at the tattoos on display in the window. All are frightened to have one done, except Sid, who says if he had the money he would have one done on both arms, not just one. This stirs the gang into having a quick reckon-up, finding that they have just enough to test Sid’s bravery.

Talking about Sid, his face seems to have taken a turn for the worse, but not wanting to appear frightened, he accepts the cash.

‘No need for us all to wait. You lot head for the seafront and I’ll see you there.’

The gang make sure Sid goes into the shop and then head for the beach. Now Sid’s legs seem to forget they’re for standing on, and he slithers down the wall and slumps into a chair.

‘Right, lad. Which one do you want?’ growls Willy.

Sid looks up. ‘Is, is, is there any tattoos you can’t do, mister?’

‘Yes, parrots,’ snarls Willy. ‘I hate the things.’

‘What, what a pity,’ replies Sid. ‘That’s just the one I wanted.’

With that, he jumps to his feet and rushes off through the door, not stopping until he’s well out of sight of Willy’s torture parlour, stopping for a breather outside Annie Seeds’ sweet shop. In the window is the answer to Sid’s problem: Biggablow Bubblegum, with two free transfers in every packet.

That’s it, and in two shakes of a donkey’s tail Sid’s strolling down to the gang, sleeves rolled up, with a huge ship on one arm and a vicious pirate on the other. The Mob all agree that this is the bravest act done by any member of the gang, except the time that Captain Wink was sent into the barbecue shop to get the Boss a roast leg of chicken. Well, he fell asleep as usual, and instead of getting the roasted chicken he set out for, he nearly got roasted himself.

While everyone’s busy congratulating Sid, Captain Wink has a plan to get his helping of shrimps and starts to whisper to Bandy, who then does a curious thing. He takes off his trilby, allows Captain Wink to hop inside it and then replaces it on his head. He then walks up to Salty Sam’s shrimp stall, gently takes off his hat again and places it by an extra-large bowl of shrimps.

Bandy then starts to ask Salty the silliest questions, like how does the tide know what time it is, so it can come in and out? While Sam is trying to answer Bandy, Captain Wink’s head keeps popping out from under the hat and getting a beakful of shrimps. Meanwhile, the rest of the gang are curious as to what Bandy’s up to, and why has Captain Wink disappeared?

The Boss takes his smart trilby off and puts it next to Bandy’s on the counter. By now the Captain is full right up to his tin mug with shrimps, and sticking his feet out, decides it’s time to make a move and starts to creep away from the now empty shrimp tub.

The Boss looks at the hat with disbelief and imagines that some giant crab is on the rampage, living in disused trilby hats. He raises his brolly and brings it down with a crash. Just for good measure he does it again on his own hat, just in case there’s a crab squatting in that. Captain Wink, thinking that a runaway steamroller has flattened Salty’s stall, emerges dazed from under his hideout.

The game’s up now. Sam has realised that this is all a huge trick to de-shrimp him. His neck swells up, and his eyes bulging with anger, he grabs his cockle-bashing mallet and decides to compensate for his loss of profit by playing the Sailor’s Hornpipe with it on the gang’s unwilling heads. That is, until they scarper rather sharpish back down to the beach. They’ve never been much for music anyway, and they sit on the sand counting the bumps on their heads.

All one each, except Captain Wink, who says he has two, forgetting that he’s still in brolly reach.

‘Wrong,’ says the Boss, who makes it three. ‘That’s for making me ruin my best trilby, you bald-headed buzzard.’

Shamus at last lets the gang into his plan for raising some cash. He’s noticed that the deckchair attendant is not around to collect his money.

‘Right, Titch, take the bus ticket machine, nip round and give everyone in a deckchair a ticket and ask for 20p.’

This seems to go down extraordinarily well with the holidaymakers. So well, in fact, that some of them are delighted and ask Titch for more than just one ticket. However, he doesn’t mind how many they want, and in no time at all has enough money for all the gang’s needs.

At last Shamus has had a successful idea, and they all settle down to ice cream, hot dogs and lots of bottles of lemonade, etc.

Sid decides to go for a paddle and is enjoying himself until Bandy, complete with snorkel, mask and flippers, well, one flipper and one converted water bottle, comes charging down the sand. With a yell similar to the one Tarzan would make when he dives off a cliff into 40 feet of water, Bandy tries to do the same and dives full length.

However, the only difference between Tarzan and Bandy is 39 feet 6 inches of water, because his eyes are so bad he has dived in behind Sid and the water still hasn’t reached his ankles. The splash of sand and seaweed up Sid’s legs really puts the willies up him, as this is as far out to sea as he’s been. He topples over.

Bandy reckons swimming is not as much fun as he first thought and hurries back to the gang, followed closely by Sid.

Captain Wink, now recovered, shouts, ‘Sid, your ship’s lost one of its masts and seems to be sinking!’

‘Yes, and look at your pirate. His head’s fell off,’ growls the Boss.

Sid, realising that what little bit of sea he’s been in has started to wash off his tattoos, gurgles, ‘Well, Willy Turt must have used the wrong ink. I’ll bash him when I see him again.’

However, the gang, not quite accepting Sid’s explanation, start to gather themselves up to do a bit of bashing themselves, namely Sid. But before they can do this, something else is going to be explained.

The reason why Titch sold so many tickets for deckchairs at 20p is that the tickets which were in the machine off the bus had ‘Sillysea 50p return’ on them, and the Sillysea holidaymakers were now bringing their deckchairs back and hoping to make a profit on them.

Gathering their belongings up as quickly as possible, our Mob decide to do the only decent thing and scarper as fast as their legs will carry them. They only manage to shake their pursuers off when they reach the bus, where there is no one around except an owl which is perched high up in a tree, looking down at the gang, wondering just what they’ve been up to this time.

The End.
