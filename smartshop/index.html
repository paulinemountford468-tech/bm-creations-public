SMARTSHOP

A compact retail idea with room to grow

Original concept by Brian Mountford
SMARTSHOP • ORIGINAL ARCHIVE NOTE

A new way to retail products

What Brian’s note describes

  • A sales unit on wheels, designed to create a larger selling area from a smaller floor footprint.
  • Special hinges allow metal doors to be fitted and units to be joined together.
  • Connected units can be secured in a locked position.
  • An operator can open or close the unit or units in seconds.

“Maximum sales area … out of the minimum of floor space used.”

These details are transcribed from the surviving SmartShop archive note.

SMARTSHOP • HOW UNITS WORK TOGETHER

Compact when closed. Wider when open.

The later foldable retail sheets show two matching units joined together. The concept is to keep a small footprint when closed, then open the units to create a broader retail display.

SMARTSHOP • FLEXIBLE RETAIL

One idea. Many possible layouts.

The original note proposes

  • Joining multiple units in different shapes to fit the available floor space.
  • Using separate units for different operators and product ranges.
  • Four operators sharing a 12 ft × 12 ft floor area, with examples including sunglasses, perfume, audio-visual products and confectionery.

A display for different traders

Later presentation pages suggest gifts, novelties, toys, crafts, seasonal goods and products from small businesses.

SMARTSHOP • POSSIBLE LOCATIONS

Bring a shop into more kinds of space

Places named in the archive note

  • Shopping malls and indoor markets
  • Airports and railway stations
  • Supermarkets and other secure retail outlets
  • Vacant retail premises that could become mini-markets

Later presentation pages add

  • High streets and empty shops
  • Public places, stations and tourist areas
  • Supermarket entrances and exits
  • Shopping centres, seasonal sales and local maker displays
SMARTSHOP • LATER RETAIL PRESENTATIONS

Features shown in the later sheets

  • Lockable, foldable retail units
  • Compact to store and easier to move
  • Attractive illuminated header panels
  • Branding in different colours
  • Manufacture as a production-line retail unit
  • A wide range of product and location options

The later presentation brochure describes the display as a working prototype and presents SmartShop as an option for start-ups and small traders.

Archive record: Brian’s autobiography says that he built a SmartShop and applied for a patent. This records an application, not a granted patent.
